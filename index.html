MRO overview

MRO stands for Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul: everything that keeps an aircraft safe, legal and ready to fly. An aircraft may only fly when it is airworthy, and an approved MRO is the organisation that proves it.

MaintenanceScheduled inspections, servicing and checks done to prevent faults.
RepairFixing defects, damage and unserviceable parts, within approved data.
OverhaulFull strip, inspect, restore and test of components and engines to a set life.
Release to serviceA licensed engineer signs a Certificate of Release to Service (CRS) before the aircraft flies.

Why it matters

Maintenance is the main barrier between a defect and an accident. Every task must be traceable and done to approved data.

Who needs it

Airlines, cargo operators, business jets, helicopter and general aviation owners, and lessors.

What it runs on

Approved manuals, certified staff, calibrated tools, genuine parts and a quality system audited by the regulator.

MRO operations

The main kinds of work an MRO performs, from the ramp to the engine shop.

Line maintenance

Turnaround checks, daily and weekly checks, defect rectification and minimum equipment list (MEL) deferrals at the airport.

Base maintenance

Heavy checks (A, C and D-type), structural inspections, modifications and cabin refurbishment in a hangar.

Engine shop

Borescope inspection, module repair, full overhaul and test-cell runs for engines and APUs.

Component shop

Repair and overhaul of landing gear, hydraulics, avionics, wheels and brakes, and fuel and pneumatic units.

NDT & structures

Non-destructive testing (eddy current, ultrasonic, X-ray, dye penetrant) plus sheet-metal and composite repair.

Modifications & AOG support

Service bulletins, airworthiness directives, retrofits, and rapid aircraft-on-ground response.

How a maintenance job flows

PlanningCheck scope from the maintenance programme, ADs and SBs.
InductionAircraft arrives; work packages and parts are prepared.
ExecutionTechnicians perform tasks; each is stamped on task cards.
InspectionIndependent inspection of critical tasks.
Release & recordsCRS issued; records are updated and kept.

Regulatory bodies and standards

MRO work is lawful only under an approval from the aviation authority. Pick a regulator to see how its rules map to MRO operations.

How the regulatory chain works

ICAO sets global standards›National authority (DGCA) writes rules›MRO is approved and audited›Certifying staff are licensed›Aircraft released to service

Bilateral agreements let an MRO hold more than one approval, for example DGCA plus EASA or FAA, so it can serve foreign-registered aircraft. Always check the current revision of each rule on the regulator’s official site before relying on it.
